# PART I - TEAM GUIDE

## 1. The assignment in one paragraph
Extend HMBC2020 into a **workshop-paper-grade** project in the **information-security** domain, in a direction **different from JANUS** (so not: Fabric implementation, Vault-based enforced single point of access, request queues, voting governance, attack trees, benchmarks) and **not** the JANUS-hardening cryptography ideas we already judged non-novel (threshold or distributed gatekeeper key, capability-token-bound decryption, verifiable outsourced decryption, curve migration, aggregate-signature voting, Raft-to-BFT swap). Proof-of-concept scale: 2-4 laptops (about 14 GB RAM each, no GPU, no cloud), about **8 weeks**, no real patient data. Syllabus hooks: blockchain security and privacy, MPC, digital-signature variants, access control in hierarchical systems, AI/ML security, PQC (`course/Course_Content.md`).

## 3. The cases - condensed plans and grounding
Each case lists: idea, verified evidence, closest prior art with identifiers and our delta, what we would build, how we would evaluate it, an 8-week outline, kill/switch triggers, venues. **Full detail is in Part II** (E+F+G: Section 9.2; D: Section 9.1; A: Sections 6-8; B and C: Section 9 table; evidence: Sections 5, 13, 14).

### 3.1 Case E+F+G - "Breaking the assumptions of HMBC and JANUS: analysis and hardening" (RECOMMENDED)
**Three questions.** (E) Do the papers' headline properties - "single point of access that cannot be bypassed", "every request logged, successful or not", "majority-honest governance", "efficient revocation" - hold under *their own* threat models? (F) What survives if stakeholders and operators are **Byzantine** rather than honest? (G) What do revoked users and removed stakeholders keep - is there **forward or backward secrecy**?
**The assumptions we test (table S1-S10 in Part II Section 9.2; quotes from the papers verified by an AI session [V]):** isolation protects the key store from node operators (HMBC V-A1); honest majority per domain and for the PBC (V-A1); Raft lacks Byzantine tolerance (HMBC's generic PoS claim is underspecified); no explicit semi-honest-stakeholder restriction was found in HMBC; revocation by gatekeeping, not cryptography; temporal ABE keys live in the key store; PBC-to-DBC "verified attributes" are trusted; JANUS analyses unauthorized access in 6.1 and also discusses management/storage in 6.2/6.3; Lewko-Waters is secure.
**Verified evidence (static readings; none has been run):**
- **[V] The domain chain does not authenticate the proxy chain's decision**: `ACSC.policyEnf` takes `verified_attributes` and `KSSC.requestData` takes `data_id`/GID as plain arguments with no caller check (`ACSC/lib/acsc.js:75-100`, `KSSC/lib/kssc.js:43-48`); proxy-chain chaincodes need all four organisations to endorse (`AND`), domain-chain chaincodes need one (`OR`) (`configs/organizations_info/chaincodes.json`).
- **[V] Unauthenticated governance finalisers**: `TMSC.majorityUpdate` (and `LSC.majorityUpdate`) apply a caller-supplied `electionApproved` with no caller check (`TMSC/lib/tmsc.js:516-545`); **[V]** `PSC.updateFromDBC` logs a caller-supplied `approved` flag (`psc.js:589-642`).
- **[V] Quorum needs zero votes in small domains**: `Math.round(n/2)` over the *other* members (`PSC/.../election.js:76-77`); the shipped topology has 2 organisations per domain.
- **[V] The key store's Vault role is the full admin policy** (sysdecrypt, `decrypt/+` for any GID, keygen), and the plugin ignores the issuing authority for role attributes, so any organisation admin can mint role keys (`vault_values.json`, `keygen_path.go:136-139`).
- **Static observation:** no epoch mechanism was identified in the inspected key path. Forward/backward-secrecy guarantees require definitions of compromise, join/leave events and old ciphertext access; absence of an epoch string is not a security proof.
- **[V] Platform semantics**: a `Shim.error` returned from a contract method counts as a *success* in `fabric-shim` 2.4.1 and does not roll back state; Fabric transaction time is the client's clock with no skew bound (class-known: CVE-2024-45244).
- **[V] Plaintext logs**: invoker, certificates, data ids written in clear to every peer's world state (`psc.js:469-497`, `lsc.js:78-101`); **[V]** raw per-hospital statistics counts (`clinics.py:175-201`).
- **Design-level (2020 text), 26 attacks, 13 rated HIGH** [O agent reading], and a **claims audit** showing nine of the paper's own claims are not supported by its design: patient-scoped temporary access, bypass avoidance, "every request logged, successful or not", forensics under collusion, policy changes "globally detected", "colluding stakeholders cannot affect contract execution", scalability, statistical privacy, Table 1's "all types of queries" (no query mechanism exists).
- **Checked and found sound (for fairness):** the Lewko-Waters core is faithfully implemented; the DB API uses an ORM (no SQL injection found); no real secrets are committed; signed-challenge voting blocks initiators and double votes.
**Closest prior art (class level only) and our delta.** No instance-level analysis of HMBC2020 or JANUS found (58+ web searches, all 72 citing papers screened, OpenAlex full text, GitHub/CVE, theses in English/Greek/Chinese) [U]. *Class-level work exists:* Fabric attack-surface and chaincode-vulnerability papers - Brotsis et al. (arXiv:2109.03574 [O]), Li et al. EASE 2022 (doi 10.1145/3530019.3531342 [O abstract]), Chen et al. ISSRE 2024 (doi 10.1109/issre62328.2024.00060 [O abstract]), GoLiSA SAC 2025 (doi 10.1145/3672608.3707728 [O abstract]); **CVE-2024-45244** (Fabric does not verify request timestamps; fix commit `155457a6`); log forgery - Phantom Events (arXiv:2502.13513 [O]); MA-ABE authority trust and collusion - Meamari et al. (arXiv:2002.07811 [S]), Datta-Komargodski-Waters (Eurocrypt 2023 [S]); cross-chain relay trust - Zamyatin et al. (ePrint 2019/1128 [O]); BFT for Fabric is *not* new - Sousa-Bessani-Vukolic (DSN 2018 [S]), Fabric v3.0.0 `configtx.yaml` offers `BFT` [V by the verifier]; forward/backward-secure MA-ABE exists - Bkakria et al. (J. Computer Security 2023 [S]). **So the paper must never claim a new attack class.** The claim is: **an executed, formally modelled analysis of two named systems (firstness is unresolved), a claims audit of their own assertions, and tested repairs.** Two classes are open even at class level in what we could reach: cross-chain policy-change detection, and `Shim.error` semantics.
**What we would build.** (1) A consolidated weakness catalogue and claims re-audit mapped to "JANUS inherits / fixes / changes"; (2) a **chaincode-level test harness** (mocha + sinon stubs of `ctx.stub` and `ClientIdentity`; the repo's chaincodes declare these dev-dependencies but ship **no tests**, so we write them), a Vault-policy test on a stock dev container, a **Solidity/Hardhat re-implementation of the six HMBC contracts** for the design-level attacks, and optionally a small two-organisation Fabric network; (3) **formal models with counterexamples**: ProVerif/Tamarin for registration and the three-party access flow, TLA+ for log completeness, governance and the Byzantine fault model; (4) quantification: Monte-Carlo governance capture and zero-vote corner cases, which guarantees survive f Byzantine operators (node counts per domain), key-lifecycle experiments; (5) **repairs implemented and measured**: authenticated cross-layer calls with signed attestations, per-function Vault policies, strict-majority quorum and caller-authenticated finalisers, `MAJORITY` endorsement, an proposed epoch lifecycle (forward/backward secrecy and ciphertext compatibility must first be specified and proved), log-before-grant with two-phase finalisation, anchors with sequence numbers and expiry, k-of-n co-signed `onDuty` tickets (case D's mechanism).
**The general lesson to state up front** (this is what makes top venues accept the paper shape): *a hierarchical access-control architecture must (1) authenticate the cross-layer decision, (2) base governance on cryptographic rather than assumed majorities, and (3) give keys an epoch lifecycle* - each backed by an executed attack, a formal counterexample and a measured repair.
**8-week outline (3 students; lean 8-9 person-weeks).** W0 decisions, reading, disclosure plan, choose 8-10 weaknesses to demonstrate (not all ~40) - W1 threat model + consolidated catalogue + harness set-up - **W2 reproduce the top findings at mock-stub level; GO/NO-GO: at least 3 HIGH findings (and ~6 of the 10 chosen) reproduce** - W3 Vault tests, policy-parser bug, Solidity re-implementation - W4 (optional) two-organisation Fabric network for end-to-end confirmation - W5 key-lifecycle and Byzantine-operator model; TLA+ and ProVerif/Tamarin models - W6 repairs; send the private advisory to the authors - W7 measurements (attack success before/after; overhead) - W8 writing and artifact.
**Kill / shrink triggers:** a published systematic analysis found (-> switch); fewer than 3 HIGH findings reproduce in W2 (-> design-level + formal parts only, or switch); instructors reject "analysis and hardening" (-> D or A).
**Venues:** CODASPY 2027 (paper **23 Nov 2026**, 12 pp - verified on the official site; a **6-page Dataset/Tool track** that accepts "exploit implementations and proof-of-concept attacks"; posters 15 Feb 2027), ARES 2027 main (Mar 2027, inferred), ASIACCS 2027 cycle 2 (**11 Dec 2026**; needs a formal core and a general lesson; "primarily blockchain" papers discouraged), BSCI 2027 (inferred Feb-Apr 2027), SECRYPT 2027 (verified 16 Feb / 25 Mar 2027), AsiaJCIS 2027 (16 Apr 2027, tentative) as the low-stakes fallback. The two Nov/Dec 2026 dates are realistic only for the lean version.
**Honest caveats.** All findings are static readings; "confirmed by reading" is not "exploited", and reachability under Fabric channel policies is inferred. Several items are prototype/deployment defaults, not design flaws (reviewers weight design flaws more: the strong ones are the unauthenticated cross-layer call, the Vault role, role-key minting, the unauthenticated finalisers with zero-vote quorums, and the missing key lifecycle). The repairs are known primitives, so defensive novelty is thin by design.

### 3.2 Case D - case-scoped, independently attested emergency tickets (ALTERNATIVE, rank 2)
**Idea.** HMBC2020's Rule 2 is formalised as `IF a.isDoctorAtHospital(*) AND a.onDuty(*) THEN X.allow(a, MHR(b)), forall MHR(b) in DB_X` - no emergency-case predicate (Sec. III-C4) [V]; a TempACL entry is (GID, role, period) signed by one CA-Admin (III-C6) [V]; in JANUS `onDuty` is a work-shift calendar and one CA-Admin can rewrite the list [O]. **Mechanism:** an emergency ticket bound to (patient, window, clinician, declared case), released only after k signed attestations from distinct, independent authorities drawn by on-chain randomness; the ticket is self-authenticating, so the domain chain verifies it **without trusting the Inter-BC relay**; the contract also emits a patient notification, records a time-boxed review, and counts a per-GID quota; degraded mode for liveness. Plain k signatures only (no gatekeeper-key thresholding).
**Prior art and delta.** Al Amin et al., SECRYPT 2025, doi 10.5220/0013527000003979 [O full text] (single in-house senior approver, multi-signature, patient notification, audit; no ticket/expiry, no formal analysis); EACMS, IEEE Access 2019, doi 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2917976 [O]; de Oliveira et al., Ann. Telecommun. 2020, doi 10.1007/s12243-020-00759-2 [O]; Alcaraz et al., SECRYPT 2025, doi 10.5220/0013559000003979 [O]; **Red Hat US 12,160,427 B2** (priority 2022-09-28; generic cloud break-glass with ledger-collected co-authorisation and a time-bound token) [O]; CoSi witness cosigning, arXiv:1503.08768 [O]. **Delta:** case-scoped ticket + *independent cross-domain* attesters + domain-side verification + a **ProVerif/Tamarin proof** (0 break-glass papers with ProVerif/Tamarin found [U]) + blast-radius analysis under f captured authorities.
**Evaluation.** ProVerif correspondence queries (release implies ticket in window with k distinct attestations; ticket for P cannot unlock P'; replay/expiry; release implies notification); hypergeometric bound C(f,k)/C(N,k) on all-k-attesters-controlled, checked by Monte Carlo; exposure vs the 2020 baseline (all patients in the domain); latency vs the paper's 603 ms; liveness under attester outage. **8-week outline:** W1-2 protocol + threat model + ProVerif; W3 contract + ACSC integration (Python simulator or Solidity on a dev chain); W4 reconciliation + gate; W5 Synthea-based labelled access-log generator; W6 experiments; W7 optional detector-lite; W8 paper. **Effort ~10 person-weeks [U].** **Kill:** a published co-attested cross-domain break-glass paper; ProVerif property collapses to "k signatures verify"; k=3 attestation p95 latency above 2-5 s on a LAN. **Venues:** SECRYPT (but Al Amin and Alcaraz appeared there), ARES/SecHealth, BSCI, AsiaJCIS. **Full plan:** Section 9.1.

### 3.3 Case A - ledger-enforced privacy-loss accounting for cross-hospital statistics (ALTERNATIVE, rank 3)
**Idea.** HMBC2020 says insurers' and researchers' statistical access is safe because "all access is controlled and logged by the PBC" (III-F-2) [V]; the paper has no DP/MPC/anonymisation mechanism [V grep]; JANUS ships raw per-hospital counts with free clinic/date filters [V]. We would enforce a **cumulative, patient-level privacy budget across hospitals and independent authorities**: the PBC debits the budget **before** any statistical release, and each hospital verifies the committed debit on its **own** PBC replica (every HMBC stakeholder runs a PBC node, [V]) before contributing a share of the answer - so the untrusted Inter-BC relay is off the safety path.
**Prior art (the idea "a ledger gates a DP budget" is NOT new).** SAP patent US 10,380,366 (2017); Yang, Margheri, Hu, Sassone, IEEE Cloud Computing 2018; Zhao et al., IEEE IoT-J 2021 (arXiv:2001.09259; the contract rejects queries when the budget is insufficient); **Mangala et al., FGCS 2026 (arXiv:2512.10426; a pre-query "Privacy Budget Ledger" contract in a hospital consortium - the closest)**; Big Bird, SOSP 2026 (arXiv:2506.05290; global budget and Sybil depletion, web setting); **Liu and Li, arXiv:2606.16763 (June 2026; person-level cross-silo DP with a sketched ledger-committed count; announces a companion paper - watch arXiv)**; Halo/WFA Privacy Budget Manager (production MPC + per-custodian charge-before-fulfilment, advertising); Zhang et al. BDMA 2024 and Jiang et al. 2022; Hybrid-TPR-ABE and Qiu 2026 (closed access, accounting granularity unknown); real-world networks (SHRINE 2009; PCORnet/ACT, Yu and Weber JMIR 2020) use masking + contracts + audit and **no cumulative privacy loss** - A's strongest *motivation*. **Surviving delta:** a **soundness definition + measured attacks + construction** for accounting units that cut across mutually distrusting custodians (a patient held by several hospitals) **and** across independent, possibly corrupt identity authorities (HMBC has no root CA; Big Bird's own Limitations section suggests registering invokers with "trusted authorities" [V]).
**Design (summary; Section 7).** Patient-level zCDP; patients optionally split into groups by a keyed hash of a common patient ID (default G=1); multiplicity-capped weights from pairwise private set intersection (the "linked" mode) or an "unlinked" fallback; distributed discrete-Gaussian noise calibrated to honest hospitals; pairwise-mask secure aggregation posted on-chain; per-issuing-authority quota reservations; refunds only if every hospital abstained; TLA+ model with three deliberately broken variants. **Evaluation:** five attacks (overlap, hospital-subset differencing, cross-authority Sybil, receipt replay, depletion) against five baselines; membership-inference advantage vs the analytic bound; Synthea data. **Numbers (recomputed, hidden assumptions stated in Section 7.5):** for a count of ~1,000 at epsilon 2, three hospitals, one corrupt: about **338 / 225 / 37** useful queries (central / linked / unlinked) *in total for all analysts over the lifetime budget*; cells of 100-200 patients allow only ~2-9 queries. **Weaknesses to resolve (W-1..W-6):** keyed-hash groups, PSI weights, honest-party noise assumption, abstain-refund liveness, per-authority reservations, mask aggregation needing at least 2 honest hospitals. **Effort 10-13 person-weeks [U]; week-4 go/no-go gate on utility.** **Kill:** the Liu-Li companion with hospitals; Hybrid-TPR-ABE full text showing multi-custodian debit-before-release. **Venues:** SECRYPT, IWPE (inferred), CODASPY. **Full plan:** Sections 6, 7, 8.

### 3.4 Case B - sealed, need-to-know global access log (SECTION INSIDE E, or fallback)
**Idea and evidence.** The inspected JANUS log stores invoker, certificates, data identifier and timestamp in replicated state; patient-identifying content depends on the request. HMBC describes global logging but has conflicting detail/hash descriptions including insurers and manufacturers (HMBC Sec. III-D4, V-A5 [V]; JANUS code writes invoker, certificates and data ids in clear, `psc.js:469-497` [V]; Du et al. 2024 (doi 10.1016/j.compeleceng.2024.109547) states the on-chain-log disclosure problem [V]). **Mechanism (SEAL-LOG):** each PBC log entry holds an unlinkable tag, the identity encrypted for opening by a quorum of stakeholders, the details encrypted for the target domain's quorum, and a proof of validity; domain chains anchor Merkle roots of their tags per epoch so anyone can check completeness; every opening is itself logged (decryption shares posted on-chain). Frame the opening key as an *audit* key (not a gatekeeper key) to stay outside the excluded zone.
**Prior art.** Harpocrates, arXiv:2211.04741 [O full text]; zkLedger, NSDI 2018 [O]; zkCross, ePrint 2024/888 / USENIX Security 2024 [O abstract]; DTRAC, doi 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3225439 [O]; ZK-EHR, doi 10.3390/systems14040385 [O]; VeilAudit, arXiv:2510.12153 [O abstract]; Odelu et al., doi 10.1145/3807407.3807473 [O abstract]; **unread:** EHRAuditChain (*Information Sciences* 736:123109, doi 10.1016/j.ins.2026.123109), InvisiReveal (JISA 2024), the 2022 LNCS threshold tag-based-encryption paper. **Delta:** two opening scopes + a cross-layer completeness reconciliation in a hierarchical access-control system. **Evaluation:** game-based definitions (log indistinguishability, non-frameability, completeness, opening accountability); ProVerif `choice[]` model for unlinkability; log write latency/size and audit-query cost on synthetic events. **Effort 9-11 person-weeks [U].** **Verdict REFRAME ~35%**: the pieces exist; read the three unread papers before choosing B. **Full plan:** Section 9 table.

### 3.5 Case C - governance-capture analysis (SECTION INSIDE E, or a pivot)
**Idea and evidence.** Domains are formed by organisation *type*, not random sampling, and admission is decided inside the domain while every member is also a PBC node (HMBC V-B1 "without engaging members of other domains"; V-A1 honest-majority assumption shared with the PBC [V]); JANUS: only the initiator's domain votes on adding a stakeholder, quorum `Math.round(n/2)` over the other members, so a one-member-other domain needs zero approvals [V]. **Back-of-envelope (invented 28-organisation registry; [U]):** the historical script estimates about 24-26 sham members under an invented equal-vote application model. It uses total domain size, unlike JANUS’s electorate excluding the initiator. Application admission alone does not change Fabric MSPs, orderers or endorsement policies, so this is not demonstrated PBC consensus capture. The N=2, one-corrupt-member corner already violates strict honest majority. **Prior art:** Hafid et al., arXiv:2104.07215 [O] (random assignment); Bano et al., *SoK: Consensus in the Age of Blockchains*, arXiv:1711.03936 [O] (static, policy-driven assignment is the norm in permissioned systems); BMS reconfiguration attack, arXiv:2109.03913 [O abstract]; ConsortiumSec, doi 10.1145/3699965 [O abstract]. **Delta:** a quantitative capture analysis of type-partitioned two-layer hierarchies (none found in 25+ phrasings [U]) + a model-checked mitigation (cross-domain admission quorum, timelock, witnesses) with its liveness cost. **Effort 4-5.5 person-weeks [U].** **Verdict SURVIVES, medium-low**; main risk is weight - use as a section of E (as the "honest majority is not enforced" result) or as a pivot paper. **Full plan:** Section 9 table, Section 7.8.

## 7. The project

### 7.1 Research questions
- **RQ1** Which natural accounting designs for HMBC-style statistical access are unsound or DoS-able, and by how much (membership-inference advantage vs number of queries)?
- **RQ2** Can a debit-before-contribute filter, verified by each custodian on its own PBC replica, give a *sound* cumulative patient-level budget with no trusted curator and no trust in the Inter-BC API?
- **RQ3** What is the price of soundness: utility (error vs epsilon; useful queries before exhaustion), latency, gas, and the cost of *not* linking patients across hospitals?
- **RQ4** How does the guarantee degrade if the PBC's governance is captured (the candidate-C tie-in)?

### 7.2 Contributions (as they would appear in the paper)
- **C1** A taxonomy and demonstration of natural-but-unsound accounting designs for HMBC-style statistical access (A1 overlap, A2 hospital-subset differencing, A3 cross-authority Sybil GIDs, A4 receipt replay, A5 depletion).
- **C2** A debit-before-contribute privacy filter on the PBC: patient-level accounting with an overlap rule; secure aggregation with noise calibrated to honest hospitals; local verification of debits on each hospital's own replica; quotas reserved per issuing authority.
- **C3** Definitions and analysis: budget-soundness game, no-release-without-committed-debit, availability under quotas, auditability, degradation under capture - invariants checked in **TLA+/TLC** including three deliberately broken mutants.
- **C4** The measured price of soundness (cost of not linking patients, collusion penalty, quota fragmentation, latency/gas on a PoC).

### 7.3 System model: what changes in HMBC (full detail: `research/reports/R2_design_budget_accountant.md`, Sec. 2)
| HMBC component | Change |
|---|---|
| RSC / TMSC | Unchanged (each hospital's *stats executor* registers as a device principal). A TempACL entry can carry an IRB-approval attribute for researchers. |
| PSC | New entry point `requestStat(Query)`; still the single entry point; `msg.sender` = GID. |
| LSC | Logs the request; budget events form the *privacy-loss log*; `retrieveLog`/majorityConsent stays the auditor path. |
| **BudgetLedger** (new, PBC) | Per-group filter, quotas, debit, share posting, release, refund. |
| **ACSC-Q** (new, hospital DBC) | A pure policy function over verified attributes plus audit logging. |
| KSSC, ABE | **Untouched** - the statistics path never decrypts records for the requester. |
| Inter-BC API | Off the safety path: used only for fan-out/throttling, trusted for **liveness only**. |

Key idea: each hospital verifies a debit *on its own PBC and DBC nodes* - it reads the transaction and contract state from its local node, recomputes the query id, re-runs validity at the observed head (replica lag and revocation ordering still need specification), and evaluates the domain's query policy locally. This uses a fact about HMBC that is [V] (every stakeholder runs a PBC node) rather than a new trusted component.

### 7.4 Threat model
Malicious analysts (any number of GIDs, adaptive queries, arbitrary hospital subsets, collusion); Sybil GIDs from k corrupt or careless authorities; colluding principals inside one organisation; up to t corrupt hospitals (semi-honest in the PoC; discussed for malicious); a fully malicious relayer/Inter-BC API (liveness only); receipt replay; depletion DoS; PBC governance/consensus capture (Section 7.8). Soundness needs **L** (ledger safety, e.g. fewer than n/3 Byzantine QBFT validators; HMBC itself assumes honest majority) and **P** (parameter integrity - budget B, collusion bound t, noise formula and contract code hash cannot change without each honest hospital agreeing). *Four logical validator owners are proposed. Co-hosting two validators on L1 is not an independent failure or compromise domain: L1 failure stops a 4-validator network requiring 3; L1 compromise can exceed f=1. This topology may measure performance but cannot validate one-Byzantine-operator resilience.*

### 7.5 Accounting and noise decisions (design agent's recommendations; arithmetic recomputed by the re-check, T10)
| Issue | Decision |
|---|---|
| Unit | **Patient-level** zCDP (neighbouring datasets differ in all records of one person at all sites) |
| Overlap | Patients split into G=16 groups by a keyed hash of a **common patient ID** (needs a human decision, Q1), one filter per group; without a common ID, G=1 (still sound). **Re-check flag W-1 below suggests defaulting to G=1** |
| Sensitivity under overlap | Multiplicity-capped weights from a one-off pairwise private set intersection (PSI) per data epoch; **unlinked fallback** uses m_max=\|S\| and no PSI |
| Budget | rho-zCDP stored as an integer micro-rho ledger; epsilon reported at delta=1e-6; Renyi filter for adaptively chosen rho |
| Noise | **Distributed discrete Gaussian**, sampled locally by each hospital, calibrated to honest parties only (corrupt hospitals cannot remove honest noise); no sampling inside MPC |
| Aggregation | Pairwise-mask secure aggregation posted on-chain (linear, no MPC framework in the target tier); MP-SPDZ only as a stretch datapoint |
| Quotas | Per group: soundness budget B ⊇ domain shares ⊇ **reservation per issuing authority** ⊇ per-GID cap; plus a common pool |
| Answers | Public on the PBC, with answer reuse (identical query costs 0) - in tension with log privacy (Q2) |
| Refunds | **Only if every hospital in S abstained** (a "fewer than \|S\| shares posted" rule is unsafe: a corrupt hospital can hand its share to the analyst off-chain after the honest ones post - TLC mutant M2) |
| Time | Lifetime budget (Q3) |

**Noise-variance consequence** (c=1, same charged rho) [V recomputed, T10]: central curator 1/(2 rho); linked mode |S|/((|S|-t)·2 rho); unlinked mode |S|²/(2 rho); the linked/unlinked **variance** gap is |S|(|S|-t) = **6x variance (2.45x RMSE)** at |S|=3, t=1 and 45x variance at |S|=9, t=4.

**Utility numbers and their hidden assumptions** (recomputed by the re-check; reproduced 337.9 / 225.2 / 37.5): the figures "central ~338, linked ~225, unlinked ~37 queries" hold **only** for a count of ~1,000 (sensitivity c=1), |S|=3, t=1, epsilon=2 (rho=0.0676, delta=1e-6), error "within ±10% at 95%" taken as sigma=50 (z=2.0), the **whole lifetime budget of a patient group spent on this one query type**, and that budget **shared by all analysts combined**. Sensitivity: z=1.96 gives 352/235/39; one-sided z=1.645 gives 500/333/56; at |S|=9,t=4 linked 188, unlinked 4.2. **Cell size matters most** (linked, epsilon 2): 100 patients -> 2.3 queries; 200 -> 9; 500 -> 56; 1,000 -> 225; 5,000 -> 5,631. The zCDP->DP conversion is conservative (exact Gaussian-DP epsilon for rho=0.0676 is 1.61), worth ~1.5x more queries with a tighter conversion.

| # | Weakness | Proposed resolution / how it changes the paper |
|---|---|---|

### 7.6 Protocol (per query, summary)
Authorise (client builds the query record incl. query hash, hospital set S, rho, noise parameter, parameter epoch, nonce; dry-runs the policy for free) -> **Debit** (PSC validates the GID via TMSC, logs via LSC, BudgetLedger atomically debits the filter, the reserved authority share (else the pool) and the GID cap) -> **Receipt** (finalised block with the `Debited` event) -> **Local verification by each hospital** (recompute query id; check committed state, parameter epoch, membership in S, current-head validity, domain policy, noise parameter >= its pinned formula, |S|>t, local counter and cap; write-ahead then post, else abstain) -> **Aggregate** (weights, fixed-point vectors, local discrete-Gaussian noise, pairwise masks) -> **Contribute** (`postShare`) -> **Release** (contract sums shares; masks cancel) -> **Log** (events form the privacy-loss log; an auditor recomputes spent budgets). Replay is stopped by binding chain id, contract, GID, query hash and parameters, sorted S, parameter epoch and nonce into the query id; masks use the query id as PRF input.

### 7.7 Security properties and the proof plan
| Property | Sketch | Reduces to |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 Budget soundness** (game) | For every patient group, total rho of released runs involving an honest hospital's contribution is at most B, against adversaries controlling analysts, k authorities, t hospitals, the relayer and scheduling | Ledger safety (L+P), signature unforgeability, hash collision resistance, contract arithmetic |
| **P1' DP of the coalition's view (unproved; blocked)** | Proposed (B+tau)-zCDP for the coalition view. PSI membership outputs and public dataset sizes/roots in R2_design are not covered by the stated noise argument; adjacency, allowed auxiliary information and computational versus information-theoretic privacy must be specified | Hybrid replacing the masked sum by the ideal sum; sensitivity bound from weights; honest-noise lower bound plus the distributed-discrete-Gaussian slack; Renyi-filter composition; post-processing |
| **P2 No release without committed debit** | For every honest hospital and query id: a share is posted only if a matching committed debit exists in its replica, at most once, never after abstaining | Protocol invariant - **to be checked with TLA+/TLC** |
| **P3 Availability under quotas** | An honest authority's within-quota debit succeeds whatever others do (given ledger liveness). *Not* a guarantee that an answer is released (W-4) | Contract invariant + BFT liveness (assumed) |
| **P4 Auditability** | Spent budgets recomputable from events alone | Ledger integrity |
| **P5 Degradation under capture (unproved)** | A local counter bounds charged rho, not automatically privacy loss under forks, changed peer membership or exposed shares. **Tool-checked model: TLA+/TLC** (the properties are arithmetic/state/concurrency invariants; ProVerif/Tamarin handle sums poorly). Constants: 3 hospitals (<=1 corrupt), 2 groups, 3 GIDs from 2 authorities (1 corrupt), B=3, <=4 queries, 2 parameter epochs. **Mutants TLC must break, with the counterexample trace shown in the paper:** M1 receipt without S/epoch binding (A4); M2 refund when posted < |S|; M3 accounting keyed per hospital set (A2). Expected 10^6-10^7 states [U]. Limitation: model != Solidity code; mirror invariants as Foundry invariant tests. **Proposed two-month scope, subject to specifications and workload measurements:** definitions, P1/P3 budget-state invariants, a reduction sketch and bounded TLC with mutants. P1' and P5 require transcript/privacy proofs and are not promised deliverables. Outside the proposed scope: UC/simulation proof of the composed system, malicious-secure PSI/aggregation, unbounded proofs, QBFT security itself, timing side channels, proof of sampler-implementation correctness (mitigate with an exact integer sampler and statistical tests).

### 7.8 The capture tie-in (candidate C, as a section)
Under HMBC's rules - and under JANUS's code, where only the initiator's domain votes and the quorum is `Math.round(n/2)` [V, G-9] - a coalition can capture the **smallest** domain (1 organisation under JANUS's quorum, 2 under a strict majority) and then admit sham stakeholders to the application registry. Admission does not by itself install a Fabric MSP, change channel policies, or add a consensus voter; those transitions must be specified before claiming PBC capture. G6's back-of-envelope numbers (`research/reports/G6_calc.py`, runs in <1 s, **assumes an invented 28-organisation registry: 20 hospitals, 3 manufacturers, 3 insurers, 2 labs**) give about 24-26 sham members under a toy equal-vote application model [U], not a demonstrated capture of JANUS consensus; the historical script also uses total domain size rather than the electorate excluding the initiator. Proposed mitigation: each honest hospital **pins** (B_h, t_h, noise formula, code hash) and keeps a durable local counter. This bounds charged rho only. ### 7.9 Evaluation
**Baselines.** B0 HMBC/JANUS as-is; B1 per-hospital local DP with local ledgers; B2 trusted central curator with global budget and deduplication (utility upper bound); B3 secure aggregation without DP (PriCollabAnalysis-style [A16]). **Naive designs used as attack targets** and what they stand for: **B3'** budgets keyed by hospital set (a *constructed* counter-example; the natural per-dataset Zhao/Yang/EDH-IIoT ledger applied to a federation); **B4** per-GID budgets (DProvDB/Vinterbo/Raisaro-style, published family); **B5** an unbound PBC-signed approval receipt (a *constructed* counter-example - HMBC as-is has no statistics path or receipt); **B6** a global budget without quotas (Big Bird's depletion problem transplanted).

**Metrics.** MI advantage (best-threshold TPR-FPR of a likelihood-ratio adversary, 2,000 paired trials) vs the analytic Gaussian-DP bound (adv = 2·Phi(mu/2) − 1; about 0.15 at epsilon 2 for the full budget); relative error / RMSE vs epsilon on a 30-query workload (counts, clipped sums, histograms of <=64 cells, cell sizes ~100-5,000); useful queries before exhaustion (<=10% error at 95%) with/without quotas and under A5; weight bias (W-2); latency broken into debit / verify / aggregate / release; gas per debit and per post; bytes per query; PSI time per epoch; scaling to 9 emulated hospitals.
**Data.** Synthea synthetic EHR, 90k patients in 9 batches; site assignment: one home site, 15% at a second, 3% at a third [U; generation time/size to be measured in W1]. *Realism caveat: linked mode needs a common patient ID; Synthea's SSN field makes this look easier than any real deployment, and HMBC has no patient identity at all - listed under Limitations.* Simulator in pure Python + numpy (<1 GB RAM). The chain is needed only for A4, A5 and the cost runs.

**Hypotheses and gates (unified; epsilon values are per patient group, lifetime).**
- **H1 / Gate G1 (utility; the same test).** At epsilon <= 4 (ideally 2), linked mode answers >= 25/30 workload queries within ±10% at 95%. PASS >= 25/30 at epsilon <= 4; CONDITIONAL 15-24/30 at epsilon 4 -> histogram-first workload, report honestly; FAIL < 15/30 at epsilon 4 -> pivot.
- **H2** unlinked/linked RMSE ratio ~ sqrt(|S|(|S|-t)) (2.45 at |S|=3, t=1); fails if the weighting bias outweighs the noise gain.
- **H3** an honest authority completes >= 90% of its reserved workload under A5 (<= 10% without quotas); fails through fragmentation if 4 authorities at 20% each leave too little for any one.
- **H4 / Gate G3 (target tier only)** median end-to-end latency <= 10 s on Wi-Fi with 3 hospitals on a 4-validator Besu network.
- **H5 / Gate G2 (attacks; corrected after the audit)** the audit showed the original "advantage >= 0.9" target is unattainable with the plan's own parameters (using adv = 2·Phi(mu/2) − 1 and rho(eps) at delta 1e-6: A1 with <=3 sites gives 0.25 at epsilon 2 and 0.46 at epsilon 4; A3 at epsilon 1 per GID gives 0.81 at k=200 and needs k=310 whole independent budgets for 0.9 (real-valued crossover about 309); A2 at epsilon 1, n=9 gives 0.87 [analytic recomputation checked 2026-10-06; independence and sensitivity assumptions still require validation]). **New pass rule:** each baseline attack's measured advantage follows its analytic curve within ±0.03 and exceeds ours by a stated margin, while ours stays below the bound + 0.02; the plan reports which attacks reach 0.9 (A2 at n=9, epsilon 2: 0.997).

### 9.1 If we switch: the Candidate-D work plan (executable without new research; sources: `G37_emergency_access.md`, re-check T4)

**Working title:** *Case-Scoped, Independently Attested Emergency Tickets for a Hierarchical Multi-Blockchain.*
**Gap evidence.** HMBC2020 Rule 2 is formalised as `IF a.isDoctorAtHospital(*) AND a.onDuty(*) THEN X.allow(a, MHR(b)), forall MHR(b) in DB_X` - no emergency-case predicate, although the prose says "patients under emergency treatment" (Sec. III-C4) [V via A1/G37 reading of the text]; a TempACL entry is (GID, temporal role, period) and "Each hospital X is responsible to independently update, sign and publish TempACL_X" (Sec. III-C6); the intro rejects patient approval in emergencies (Sec. I-B), so any fix must not put a human on the clinician's critical path; forensics is post-hoc only (Sec. V-A5). In JANUS code `onDuty` is a work-shift calendar (`ACSC/lib/policies.js:28-60`, `ACSC/lib/helper.js:56-105`) and a single CA-Admin can rewrite its organisation's TempACL (`TMSC/lib/tmsc.js:216-286`) [O: A1 static reading; not re-verified by T9]. Malamas's thesis contains the same role-based `onDuty` access [E01, O].
**Mechanism.** An *emergency ticket* tau = (ticket id, clinician GID, patient pseudonym/admission id, [t0, t1], reason code, quota class) filed on the PBC through a new Emergency Smart Contract; release requires **>= k signed attestations from k distinct, independent authorities** (machine attesters, no human on the critical path), drawn per ticket by on-chain verifiable randomness from the trust-anchor set; the ticket is self-authenticating (k signatures verifiable against trust anchors), so the DBC's ACSC verifies it **without trusting the Inter-BC middleware**; the contract also emits a patient/next-of-kin notification event, records a time-boxed independent review obligation, and counts a per-GID quota; Rule 2 becomes `... AND validTicket(a, b, now)`. Plain k ECDSA/Ed25519 signatures only (no gatekeeper-key thresholding: exclusion zone 2 respected); a *degraded-ticket* mode (k'=1, lower quota, mandatory review) handles liveness. Expiry needs a trusted time anchor, because Fabric's chaincode time is the client's clock (G-11).
**Evaluation.** Threat model: A1 licensed clinician snooping; A2 rogue CA-Admin minting TempACL entries; A3 f of N authorities captured or colluding; A4 a compromised domain majority ignoring tickets; A5 ticket replay / relay / cross-patient reuse; (optional) A6-A8 detector evasion, attester poisoning, minority log tampering. Formal: **ProVerif correspondence queries** - (a) release(C,P,t) => ticket(C,P,w) with t in w and k distinct attestations (injective); (b) a ticket for P cannot unlock P'; (c) replay and expiry; (d) release => notification event; plus a hypergeometric bound on the probability that an adversary controls all k attesters drawn (C(f,k)/C(N,k)), validated by Monte Carlo. Honest limit: attestations certify plausibility, not the truth of an emergency; the guarantee is bounded blast radius, non-silent use and reviewability, not "no abuse". Metrics: exposure E(f,k,N,quota) (patients reachable, vs 2020 baseline = all patients in the domain); undetected exposure x time-to-detect; emergency-path latency vs the 2020 paper's 603 ms at 100 concurrent requests; k-vs-latency curve; liveness under attester outage. Baselines: 2020 onDuty rule; JANUS shift-calendar rule; Al Amin-style intra-organisation 2-of-2 multi-signature; EACMS patient-set rule.

### 9.2 Candidate E+F+G: "Breaking the assumptions of HMBC and JANUS" - the analysis-and-hardening work plan

**Working title:** *Who Guards the Single Entry Point? Breaking the Assumptions of Hierarchical Multi-Blockchain Access Control (HMBC and Janus): Analysis and Hardening.*

**F and G in one paragraph each.**
- **F (Byzantine stakeholders/operators).** A plain Raft-to-BFT swap is *not* novel (BFT ordering for Fabric: Sousa-Bessani-Vukolic DSN 2018; Fabric v3.0.0's `configtx.yaml` offers `BFT`, "at least 3*F+1" - verified by the verifier at the v3.0.0 tag). - **G (forward/backward secrecy).** HMBC/JANUS revoke by gatekeeping only and keep a static domain key (U8). Forward/backward-secure multi-authority ABE schemes already exist (Bkakria et al., J. Computer Security 2023; Comput. Commun. 2022; RS-HABE, IEEE TDSC 2019), so a new scheme would be incremental. What is not covered: an analysis of what a *mediated, decentralized, hierarchical-blockchain* design loses on revocation or stakeholder removal, and a proposed epoch lifecycle to investigate. **Adding SA@epoch alone is not a forward/backward-secrecy repair**: existing ciphertexts bind their original policy, new attributes do not satisfy old policy leaves, cached plaintext/partial decryptions remain usable, and a static master secret can regenerate keys. Specify ciphertext/policy updates or a proven mediated transform, key erasure, join/leave semantics and the compromise model; measure only the properties actually established.

## 15. Glossary
**HMBC / PBC / DBC** the 2020 architecture and its proxy and domain chains; **TMSC/RSC/PSC/LSC/ACSC/KSSC** its smart contracts; **GID** global identifier (an Ethereum address); **TempACL** the signed temporal-access list on the PBC; **TOCTOU** time-of-check vs time-of-use; **zCDP** zero-concentrated differential privacy; **rho / epsilon** privacy-loss parameters (epsilon reported at delta 1e-6); **MI advantage** membership-inference advantage; **PSI** private set intersection; **QBFT** the Byzantine-fault-tolerant consensus in Hyperledger Besu; **stats executor** the per-hospital process that computes a hospital's share of a statistical answer; **linked / unlinked mode** with / without a common patient ID across hospitals; **useful query** one answered within ±10% at 95%; **MVP** the Target tier (Section 8.1); **SQ1-SQ7** the overlap questions used by the re-check (ledger, debit-before-release, multiple custodians, MPC, Sybil queriers, cross-silo patients, soundness/attacks).

## 16. Ethics and safety
Synthetic data only (Synthea); no real hospital, patient or credential; no exploitation of any running system - the JANUS observations come from static code reading and are presented as design-level analysis; the PoC runs on the team's own laptops. Attacks are demonstrated on baselines inside a simulator. Limitation to state in the paper: a common patient identifier does not exist in HMBC and is only plausible in some jurisdictions.
