### 1.2 NEW facts about HMBC2020 not in the brief
- **Request carries no authentication material in Fig. 6** (PDF p.12): step (1) request = [cert_AX, data_id] only; step (4) PSC->ACSC = [GID, **verified attributes**, data_id] with no signature/MAC/expiry; step (5) ACSC->KSSC = [GID, data_id]. Registration has a proof-of-possession (III-D2) but access requests have no stated challenge/nonce. Certificates are public, so request replay/framing (log pollution under a victim's GID) is not excluded by the text.
- **Long-term-role access bypasses the KSSC in the base design.** Table 3: `requestData` (KSSC) "is activated when temporal attributes are verified". For rule 1 (long-term only) the user receives the ciphertext directly and holds all keys. The anti-bypass "System role" is only a "Remark" (III-E2, p.134403) and a bullet (V-A4). (JANUS fixes this - see matrix row N4.)
- **Table 2 `retrieveLog`**: actuator = "Auditor", "threshold enabled function"; the Auditor role is never defined (who, appointed by whom, scope of what may be read, purpose limitation).
- **GID = Ethereum address** (IV-D, p.134406). RSC links "the newly assigned GID_A and the user's certificate(s)" and logs it (III-D2); a second authority Y issues cert_AY after the user "will prove possession of the global identifier GID_A". Nothing says Y verifies that the person behind cert_AY is the person behind GID_A. GID <-> person binding is the anti-collusion root of Lewko-Waters and is delegated to unspecified out-of-band procedures.
- **Patients** appear as users (III-B) and in the Lewko-Waters policy example, but have no role in any contract; Intro I-B explicitly rejects patient approval in emergencies; there is no patient-facing audit.
- **GDPR/HIPAA** are cited as drivers (Intro, III-E) but nothing implements erasure, minimisation or accounting-of-disclosures.
- **Future work as stated** (Sec. VI, p.134411): different consensus per layer, larger-scale PoC, hybrid encryption for large objects, intra-blockchain communication / consensus consolidation / Docker orchestration. Nothing on privacy, emergency, formal analysis.
- **Table 1 comparison dimensions** (HMBC): encryption scheme; access revocation; distributed trust management; fine-grained access control; tailored forensics; adaptability; data search scheme. (JANUS Table 1 drops revocation, forensics and search and adds: access model; application environment; multi-owner setting; dynamic trust mgmt; inter-/cross-domain policy mgmt; enforces single point of access; global source of truth; artifact availability.)

### GC1 - The forensic log is a surveillance log (log/metadata privacy and linkability)  [seed a + new a2, N7, g2]
**Problem.** HMBC sells "tailored forensics" = every request, update and registration is written to a ledger replicated at ALL stakeholders. The record reveals who (GID/cert), what (data_id, which patient), where (which DBC), when - to insurers, device makers, and every other hospital. Only record content is encrypted. Needed: a log that stays *accountable* (auditors can reconstruct who accessed what) while a stakeholder who merely replicates it learns nothing beyond a bound, and a patient can verify disclosures of their own record.
**Evidence.** HMBC III-D4 (p.134402) "forcing the logging of every event"; V-A5 (p.134408) "Any access request (successful or not) ... will be logged at the PBC"; III-D2 "the link between these credentials and GID_A is logged on the PBC"; Table 2 `retrieveLog` is the only access control on the log ("Authorities grant access through majority voting"). JANUS code: `PSC/lib/psc.js:483-497` + `LSC/lib/lsc.js:78-100` store invoker, base64 certificates, dataID in cleartext in PBC world state; `faq.md:130-160` TempACL with `DOCTOR_OF` patient lists and shift calendars on the same ledger; `ACSC/lib/acsc.js:101-118` logs GID + patient identifier + decision on the DBC. JANUS retrieveLogs (`LSC/lib/lsc.js:609+`) is an API gate over data every peer already holds.
**Why JANUS does not fix it.** JANUS has no privacy requirement or section; its Table 1 only rewards a "Global source of truth". It adds *more* logged material (ACL contents, election records). Auditor access is by 30-min majority election with no purpose limitation or minimisation.
**Attack/failure scenario.** (1) An insurer's peer admin reads CouchDB directly (no need to win an election) and learns that GID-x (a named oncologist) looked up patient P's record at 02:10 from hospital H, with the doctor-patient edge also visible in `DOCTOR_OF`; repeated per patient this reconstructs patient-visit graphs and doctors' cross-hospital activity. (2) Because one GID spans all authorities (Lewko-Waters requirement), the same person is linkable across hospital, research and manufacturer roles ("Combine Identity" in `faq.md:98-106`).
**Contribution class.** Protocol design + formal analysis (accountability-vs-privacy game, unlinkability of log entries to non-auditors, threshold opening reusing the paper's own majorityConsent) + measurement (log write latency/size, audit-query cost on synthetic 10^5-10^6 events). Natural hooks in the original design: LSC, RSC, Table 2 retrieveLog.
**Kill-search for literature agents.** "privacy-preserving audit log blockchain EHR threshold opening / pseudonymous access log / accountable anonymity healthcare" and Du et al. 2024; also non-blockchain "accountable pseudonymity / searchable encryption audit logs".
**Strength / risk.** Strongest tie to the original paper's own selling point; risk = crowded privacy-audit literature and earlier lightly-explored idea (must differentiate via multi-authority GID + hierarchical PBC replication + patient-verifiable accounting).

### GC2 - Emergency access is "working hours" and is only audited afterwards  [seed b + N8]
**Problem.** The emergency rule has no incident scope, no patient/guardian visibility, and the set of "onDuty" doctors is minted unilaterally by each stakeholder's CA-Admin. Needed: emergency (break-glass) access that is (i) scoped to a declared case/patient, (ii) time- and volume-bounded, (iii) visible to the patient or their home authority, (iv) not dependent on one authority's single signature, while keeping the paper's no-manual-approval requirement.
**Evidence.** HMBC III-C4 rule 2 (formal form quantifies over `∀MHR(β)∈DB_X`, no emergency predicate); III-C6 TempACL entry = (GID, role, period); III-D1(a) "A CA-Admin may independently update any trust anchor"; I-B rejects patient approval. JANUS Access Rule 1 (J p.10) same wording; code: `ACSC/lib/policies.js:28-60` any doctor in any `DOCTOR_WORK_SHIFT` window reads any patient uuid ("dynamic" access until shift end); `ACSC/lib/helper.js:56-105`; `artifacts/janus-repo/docs/key_principals_privacy_per_entity.md` DOCTOR_ER is a "STATIC DOMAIN-WIDE" key; `TMSC/lib/tmsc.js:216-286` single CA-ADMIN rewrites its org's TempACL.
**Why JANUS does not fix it.** Neither the model (Sec. 4.1) nor the attack trees (Sec. 6.1) treat an authorised user abusing a legitimate rule or a stakeholder CA-Admin abusing TempACL; no code for notification, case binding, quotas.
**Attack/failure scenario.** A CA-Admin at the smallest member hospital adds a colluding GID to its TempACL with an all-week shift; that GID can now pull every patient record of every hospital through the proper channel, leaving only log lines that nobody analyses. Same for a curious on-duty clinician browsing celebrity records (snooping) - the breach is "authorised".
**Contribution class.** Protocol design (case-bound emergency grants, k-of-n cross-authority co-signing of TempACL entries, rate/blast-radius limits, deferred patient notification) + attack/measurement (abuse simulation on synthetic access logs; detection quality; added latency of the emergency path).
**Kill-search.** "break-the-glass access control blockchain multi-authority", EACMS (cited in JANUS Table 1 as [13], Rajput et al., IEEE Access 2019), "emergency access ABE healthcare cross-institution" 2022-26.
**Strength / risk.** High value; HIGHEST novelty risk because break-the-glass is a mature topic (Ferreira et al. 2006 line of work, many blockchain EHR emergency schemes). Differentiator would have to be the multi-authority / no-global-root / hierarchical setting.

### GC3 - Statistical access across independent authorities has no cumulative disclosure control  [seed h]
**Problem.** The ecosystem explicitly promises researchers and insurers "statistical" access across several hospitals. Each hospital answers independently; nothing bounds what a principal learns by composing many queries across authorities and time. Needed: a cumulative-leakage accounting mechanism enforceable at the single entry point and checkable from the ledger, without a trusted central curator.
**Evidence.** HMBC III-F-2 (p.134403) "Allow insurance companies to access statistical hospital treatment data. Since all access is controlled and logged by the PBC ... the privacy of personal health records is preserved." (log = control is asserted, no mechanism); III-C4 'Researcher' role; Intro I-A2 "doctors from other hospitals ... statistical medical data". JANUS code: `database_api/api/dbc_api/src/disease_stats.py:1-45` returns raw `number_of_cases` for every hospital, with optional `clinic_uuid` and free `start_date/end_date`; `artifacts/janus-repo/docs/domain_io.md:171-215`; `ACSC/lib/policies.js:115-139` only checks "researcher + active". No threshold/noise/suppression/budget anywhere (grep).
**Why JANUS does not fix it.** JANUS implements the endpoints (data_02/data_03/data_04) and reads "anonymized data" as a motivating example (J p.6) but no disclosure control; its security analysis is only about ciphertext access.
**Attack scenario (conceptual).** Differencing/tracker attack: query disease D for [t0,t1] at clinic c, then [t0,t1+1d]; the difference is the count for one day at one clinic - often 0 or 1 patient. Across hospitals the per-organisation breakdown is returned, so a rare diagnosis plus a hospital reveals membership. Because the per-GID query history is already on the PBC ledger, cumulative attacks are easy to mount and impossible to bound today.
**Contribution class.** System + protocol design + measurement + attack: (i) demonstrate differencing/reconstruction on a synthetic multi-hospital dataset via the JANUS-style endpoint shapes; (ii) design a global per-GID privacy-budget ledger kept by the PBC (the single point of access makes the budget unbypassable - exactly the property JANUS enforces cryptographically), with per-hospital local noise (distributed DP) or small-scale secure aggregation; (iii) utility-vs-epsilon measurement; (iv) show budget accounting across multiple authorities for ONE GID (Lewko-Waters GID is the natural principal).
**Kill-search.** "differential privacy budget ledger blockchain healthcare statistics", "multi-institution query auditing cumulative leakage consortium blockchain", "federated count queries DP hospitals access control layer".
**Strength / risk.** Cleanest concrete attack on a real artifact; syllabus-friendly (MPC, privacy, AI/ML security); laptop-trivial (synthetic data, counts, Laplace). Risk: DP-on-blockchain surveys exist; novelty must rest on "budget enforced by the single hierarchical entry point across independent authorities" and on a measured attack against the actual data model.

### GC4 - Governance capture in the hierarchy: domain-local membership dilutes the global vote  [seed f + f2 + N6 + N8]
**Problem.** "Honest majority in each domain" is the paper's whole trust root, but domains are formed by organisation TYPE (small, non-random) and membership is decided inside the domain while every member also votes in the proxy chain. Needed: a quantitative trust model for two-layer permissioned chains (capture probability, minimum adversary size, effect on forensics), and governance rules that bound it.
**Evidence.** HMBC V-A1 (p.134406): "for each Domain BC the majority of its stakeholders are honest. Since Domain BC stakeholders also operate PBC nodes, the same assumption holds for the PBC"; III-D1(b) "consensus of stakeholders in the corresponding domain" to add/remove a stakeholder vs V-B1 "majority of existing stakeholders"; V-A5 only claims policy changes are "globally detected", not prevented. JANUS: J p.19 "Adding/removing stakeholders within an organization ... handled at the domain level"; `TMSC/lib/tmsc.js:352,377-379` new CA must be in the initiator's domain and only that domain votes; `PSC/lib/types/elections/election/election.js:76-77` quorum `Math.round(n/2)` (n=2 -> 1 vote; n=4 -> 2 votes = 50%); one-org-one-vote across the PBC; default election window 30 minutes (line 85). Demo topology: 4 stakeholders.
**Why JANUS does not fix it.** Its threat model (J p.20) assumes a trusted majority per domain and says only that forged contracts would be "easily detected"; Raft is CFT; BFT is deferred (excluded zone: do not propose BFT swap).
**Attack scenario.** Two of three manufacturer stakeholders (a domain majority) add k sham stakeholders to the manufacturer domain; each is a full PBC voter, so the colluding bloc soon controls the PBC majority and can approve auditor access to the global log, add/remove domains, or change a cross-domain policy. Nothing in the 2020 or 2023 designs bounds per-domain share of PBC votes.
**Contribution class.** Formal/probabilistic analysis + simulation + countermeasure design (cross-layer quorum on admission, per-domain vote caps/weights, join delay + objection window, PBC veto for ACSC/policy changes). Laptop-scale: Monte-Carlo and an Alloy/TLA+ or small ProVerif-like model; a tiny prototype is optional.
**Kill-search.** "Sybil attack consortium blockchain membership governance", "hierarchical/multi-chain permissioned governance voting dilution", "committee capture sharding by organization type".
**Strength / risk.** Most certain to be rigorous and cheap; weakest "wow", and the finding is mainly about the 2020/2023 design rules (reviewers may call it analysis-only). Good as a core section combined with GC2's TempACL-abuse angle.

### GC5 - Accountability soundness of the PBC->DBC request pipeline (log-before-grant, freshness, attestation)  [seed d + e + k + N4]
**Problem.** The central security claim is "forensics-by-design: every request is logged, and the PBC check binds the later DBC action". The text and artifact show (i) logs written after completion via an off-chain cache, with responses released before block finality; (ii) the DBC acts on "verified attributes" it cannot authenticate; (iii) temporal/CRL checks are made at the PBC and used later against a possibly stale snapshot and a client-supplied clock. Needed: a formal statement of the pipeline's accountability properties (grant => durable log entry; grant => a fresh, valid, non-revoked PBC verdict) and the minimal change that makes them hold.
**Evidence.** HMBC III-D4 + IV-B (cache DB in Docker 1), V-B3 (p.134409) "a transaction can be finalized before a block is verified ... since only the transaction hash is stored in the PBC"; Fig. 6 (PDF p.12) step (4) carries "verified attributes" with no proof; Table 3 policyEnf input "verified attributes, data_id". JANUS: J p.12 "(already validated in the PBC)"; `ACSC/lib/acsc.js:75-118` and `KSSC/lib/kssc.js:44-70` perform no caller/attestation check; `PSC/lib/psc.js:497-560` log write nested in a request that returns `Shim.error` on validation failure (rejected attempts probably not committed - unverified); Fabric `GetTxTimestamp` = "client's timestamp" (OPENED pkg.go.dev).
**Why JANUS does not fix it.** Its attack tree (J Fig. 8) treats PBC and DBC as components to compromise, not as a protocol whose messages must carry proof; "successful or not" logging claim (Table 1 note 4) is stated, not demonstrated.
**Attack/failure scenario.** A compromised or malicious stakeholder Inter-BC instance (or any identity that can invoke its domain's ACSC) submits `policyEnf` with forged verified attributes for a GID of its choice; the PBC never saw the request, so no PBC log exists. Conversely, a revoked clinician's request validated at t0 is processed after the CRL update through the queue.
**Contribution class.** Formal analysis (ProVerif/Tamarin model of the three-party request protocol with accountability as correspondence/injectivity properties) + protocol repair + measurement (revocation-propagation latency distribution under the Inter-BC queue model).
**CAUTION - overlap with exclusion zone 2.** Adjacent to "capability-token-bound partial decryption", "formal definition of authorization -> transform" and "generic break-and-fix JANUS". Only viable if framed as formal accountability verification of the ORIGINAL 2020 pipeline (log completeness + freshness), with JANUS as a secondary data point, and with no ABE/token redesign as the contribution. Treat as the fallback card.

## 5. Comparison dimensions

Used by HMBC (Table 1, p.134397): encryption scheme; access revocation; distributed trust management; fine-grained access control; tailored forensics; adaptability; data search scheme.
Used by JANUS (Table 1, J p.5): access model; application environment; multi-owner setting; dynamic trust management; inter-/cross-domain policy management; enforces single point of access; global source of truth; implementation (artifact).
Neither paper compares on (candidate differentiators):
1. **What the audit trail reveals / unlinkability of logged identities** (log privacy and who can read it). Both papers count "global source of truth" as pure virtue.
2. **Scope and accountability of exceptional (emergency/temporal) access** - granularity (role vs case vs patient), who can mint it (single vs multi-authority), notification, bounded blast radius.
3. **Cumulative disclosure under aggregate/statistical queries** (budgeted leakage across independent authorities).
Secondary: formally verified / quantified security status (proof, model-checking result, or attack-capture probability); revocation latency (JANUS even removed the revocation column); post-quantum readiness; patient visibility.

## 6. Honest assessment: best bets for a workshop paper

The two strongest bets are GC3 and GC1, and the cheapest way to get a complete, defensible paper is to build the contribution around ONE of them plus a GC4-style quantitative/formal section. GC3 (statistical access) has the cleanest, most demonstrable core: a concrete, reproducible differencing attack on the JANUS artifact's actual endpoint shape, a defensible design (a per-GID cumulative-leakage ledger at the single entry point, with local noise per hospital), laptop-scale evaluation on synthetic data, and a natural syllabus fit; its weakness is that "DP plus blockchain" is crowded, so novelty must be argued on the hierarchy-specific accounting (one GID, many authorities, single entry point) and must survive a literature check. GC1 (log privacy) is the most faithful extension of the original paper because it attacks the paper's own flagship feature, and it supports both a formal game and a measured cost table, but it overlaps a previously "lightly explored" direction and a visible privacy-audit literature, so it needs the sharper multi-authority/GID/patient-accounting angle. GC4 is the safest for rigour and cost but is the least exciting by itself and risks being read as "analysis of someone else's rules"; it works best as a secondary contribution. GC2 is the most important in real life and the riskiest for novelty (break-the-glass is a mature topic), and GC5 sits in or next to the excluded zone, so use it only as a fallback or as the formal-verification component of another card. Everything above is conditional: A1 did no literature search, and the JANUS code findings are static readings (marked where unverified, notably N4 log-on-reject and the clock-skew question), so each card should go through an adversarial novelty search before commitment.
