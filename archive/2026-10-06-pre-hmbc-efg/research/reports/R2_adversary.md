## (a) Primary idea (G2's C2: ledger-enforced GLOBAL DP budget across custodians + authorities in HMBC)

**Verdict: SURVIVES-ONLY-IF-REFRAMED. Confidence ~65%.** (Coverage caveat: no Google Scholar; Elsevier journals have no abstracts in OpenAlex/S2, so they were checked by TITLE only.)

What I killed. These framings are now **dead** as contributions:
1. "Put a DP privacy budget on a (permissioned) blockchain / smart contract": Zhao et al. 2020 (+ AsiaCCS poster), EDH-IIoT (Fabric, Information Sciences), OPU-TF-IoT (IEEE IoT-J 2025), Ouyang et al. LNCS 2022.

4. "Per-querier enforcement is unsound; you need one global budget; Sybils will drain it": Big Bird (SOSP 2026).
5. "Hospital-subset differencing" as a new attack: it is a site-level tracker attack (Denning 1979). "Cross-silo patient overlap breaks per-silo budgets": already stated for FL by ULDP-FL (VLDB 2024). Per-user budgets for clinical count queries: Vinterbo et al. JAMIA 2012. Per-user, per-genome budgets in a multi-site network: Raisaro et al. JAMIA 2017. Use all of these as motivation and baselines. Do not claim them as contributions.
6. "We introduce privacy-budget ledgers for multi-site healthcare": Rahimian et al. (IEEE TPS-ISA / HMD-SPiRiT workshop, 2025/26) already names this as an agenda item. Cite it as the paper that says the problem is still OPEN.

What I could NOT kill. No paper found in arXiv, Crossref, OpenAlex (title+abstract) or S2 forward citations (Zhao, Shrinkwrap, Drynx, EDH-IIoT) does all of the following together. (i) It defines and enforces privacy-loss soundness without a trusted curator, when the accounting units cut across custodians (a patient held by several hospitals) AND across *independent, possibly corrupt identity authorities* (one principal holding GIDs from several authorities). (ii) Each custodian gates its own MPC share contribution on verifying a committed ledger debit (debit-before-share, with no replay or TOCTOU across chains). (iii) It analyses budget-depletion DoS when the Sybil source is a *corrupt authority*. Big Bird's own Limitations section says Sybil control "could require API invokers to register with trusted authorities". That is exactly the assumption that fails in HMBC's multi-authority trust model (no root CA; each stakeholder is its own CA). This is the hook.

**Honest judgement on Big Bird and Zhao (item 3).** Zhao 2020 is a single-curator, single-dataset budget counter plus answer reuse on a public ledger. It does not involve custodians, MPC, identities or adversaries. Our idea is far from Zhao *if and only if* we go beyond a counter. Big Bird is conceptually very close. Its theorem ("per-querier accounting is unsound; enforce globally; defend against Sybil depletion with quotas") is the security insight our C2 was going to claim. Our setting differs on three axes:
- data are partitioned across mutually distrusting custodians, not on a user's device;
- no single party can enforce the filter: enforcement must be distributed and verifiable;
- identities come from several independent authorities, some of which may be corrupt, rather than the open web.

A reviewer will say "Big Bird for hospitals on a blockchain" unless the paper shows that Big Bird's mechanism *cannot be applied as-is* in this setting and contributes the distributed-enforcement piece.

## (c) Minimum publishable delta (workshop)
The paper is publishable only if its headline is a **soundness result plus attacks**, not an architecture. Minimum package:
1. **A formal budget-soundness game** for the multi-custodian, multi-authority setting. The adversary corrupts up to k of m identity authorities and t of n custodians, issues adaptive queries over arbitrary hospital subsets, and replays or reorders receipts across PBC and DBC. Soundness: for every patient, the total privacy loss released about them to *any coalition of principals* is at most B. Availability: an honest authority's quota cannot be drained by other authorities.
2. **Negative results against three designs that already exist in the literature**, each with a measured attack (membership-inference advantage vs. number of queries, on Synthea data with injected overlap):
   - per-custodian/per-dataset ledgers (Zhao, EDH-IIoT, Hybrid-TPR-ABE style) fail through patient overlap and subset differencing;
   - per-GID / per-user budgets (Vinterbo, Raisaro style) fail through cross-authority Sybil GIDs minted by a corrupt stakeholder CA;
   - log-only ledgers (HMBC as-is; Qiu et al.'s "estimated" budget) fail through receipt replay, TOCTOU and release-without-debit.
   Only the third is specific to blockchains. The first two are known phenomena, re-instantiated here.
3. **A construction meeting the definition.** Debit-before-share: each custodian's MPC party verifies the committed debit itself (threshold signature or light-client proof from PBC validators), keyed to accounting units of (patient group x issuing authority x domain). The design should also include authority-scoped, Big-Bird-style quotas with an explicit bound on damage from k corrupt authorities, and noise sampled in MPC or calibrated to t corruptions. Provide a proof sketch (reduction to ledger safety, signature unforgeability, MPC security and DP composition), plus a ProVerif or Tamarin model of authorise -> debit -> receipt -> share -> release.
4. **Cost and utility numbers**: gas and latency per debit, MPC overhead, and useful queries before exhaustion with and without the depletion attack.

Without (1) and (2) the paper reduces to "Zhao + Shrinkwrap on Ethereum", which is incremental and dead. Mandatory related-work paragraph: Big Bird, Zhao, OPU-TF-IoT, EDH-IIoT, Hybrid-TPR-ABE, Qiu 2026, Rahimian 2026, DProvDB, ULDP-FL, Shrinkwrap, Drynx, Vinterbo 2012, Raisaro 2017, Denning 1979.
