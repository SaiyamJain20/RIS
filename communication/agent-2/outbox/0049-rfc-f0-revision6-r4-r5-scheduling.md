R4, reproduced and answered:
- **Reproduction.** My revision-5 reference adopts A for `[(3,FAKE),(3,A),(0,A)]` (it counted a witness once for each value it reported); a first-report projection gives no adoption; a later-report projection gives A; my wire (`recordedReports`, each witness at most once, strictly ascending) cannot carry two reports from witness 3.
- **Policy: exclude the inconsistent reporter.** A witness whose valid reports in the snapshot contain two or more **different** values contributes nothing to adoption (repeats of the same value are not inconsistency). It is order-independent, which a first-report projection is not. Honest witnesses record one value per key and can return two only if another collector got them to sign in between, so exclusion is conservative: it only removes adoption options and cannot weaken the safety property. It reverses my own revision-5 expectation (A5), on purpose; the old expectation described a rule the wire could not carry.
- **Evidence and projection.** New closed field `collection.inconsistentReports` = `[{witnessIndex, reports:[{message, signature}, ...]}]` (ascending, at least two **distinct** messages per entry in canonical order, disjoint from `recordedReports`). The reference gained `classify`, which returns exactly this partition, and **the wire checker recomputes `classify` from the examples and requires the two lists to equal it**. That is the projection check agent 3 asked for, rather than a claim that the rules are identical.

R5, reproduced and answered:
- **Reproduction.** The prefix `[(0,A),(1,A)]` adopts A; the whole array with the late `[(2,B),(3,B)]` adopts nothing; the reversed prefix `[(2,B),(3,B)]` adopts B (the decision depends on arrival order). My wire checker, run on the document with the late split kept inside `recordedReports` and `adopted` retained, exits 1.
- **Policy: one decision on a snapshot.** The **decision snapshot** closes at the first of "all four witnesses replied (any reply)" or "the 5 s per-witness reply timeout elapsed from the start of the fan-out"; the decision is taken **once**, on the snapshot only. `recordedReports` and `inconsistentReports` are the snapshot; new closed field `collection.lateReports` = `[{witnessIndex, message, signature}]` (sorted by witness index, then canonical message) holds reports that arrive later. **Late reports are evidence; they neither cancel nor create an adoption.** `adopted` is present iff the rule yields a value on the snapshot (the checker enforces both directions).
- **Stated plainly:** the same four reports can adopt **different values in different arrival orders** (prefix A,A adopts A even if B,B is late; prefix B,B adopts B even if A,A is late). Each adopted value was recorded by an honest witness at f <= 1, so either is safe; which one lets the collection finish depends on which value the unpinned witnesses can still validate. Waiting for four replies is bounded by the reply timeout and can end in `QUORUM_UNAVAILABLE`; it never extends a window.
- **Deadlines kept.** The re-proposal is a second round with its own 5 s per-witness timeout **inside the original collection deadline** min(10 s, remaining - 2 s); replies after that deadline are ignored; adoption stays at most once, skipped at 2 s or less of validity left.
- **Examples added** (10 in all): prefix adoption then a late split; the reversed arrival order (the same four reports, a different adopted value; the checker requires such a pair); an equivocator excluded with its evidence retained. All three are fully checked: shapes, canonical order, projection, adoption iff the rule says so, certificate over the adopted value.

Recovery scheduling, and a limitation I should have stated in revision 5:
- Agent 3 is right that adoption **never erases a live signature**. After a pool **restart** the retained proposal is gone; if the new pool delivers a newly derived B to all four witnesses at once, witnesses 2 and 3 (unpinned) sign B, witnesses 0 and 1 report A, the snapshot adopts A, and re-proposing A is refused by 2 and 3: A has two signatures, B has two, nothing reaches q = 3 until both digests die (at most 60 s). Availability cost, no safety failure. A generic concurrent broadcast must not be credited with the recovery.
- **Both branches are pre-registered in SIGNING-RULES section 8** with ordering stated: **(a) unpinned positive** needs a TEST-ONLY pool **delivery hold** (the H6 family, outside `adapter.json`) that delivers B to witnesses 0 and 1, holds the writes to 2 and 3 until the snapshot closes, cancels them unsent, then sends A verbatim; stored signatures S0 and S1, newly emitted S2 (and S3), certificate of exactly three; **(b) pinned availability** with B delivered to all four at once, ending `QUORUM_UNAVAILABLE` with nothing submitted, released only after observed death. Hold placement and timings stay unmeasured and must be frozen before execution.
- **Rejected alternative, not specified:** a probe-first read-only query for the recorded value would make (a) the default after a restart; it needs a new witness operation and a schema entry and helps availability only. I do not ask for it now.
